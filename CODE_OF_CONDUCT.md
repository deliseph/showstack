# Code of conduct

## The short version

This project is for people who make live events work. Crew culture at its best
is direct, competent and looks after the person next to you. That is the
standard here.

## Expected

- Be direct about the work. Be kind about the person.
- Assume the other person knows something you do not. In this field they
  usually do.
- Treat a first pull request from someone who has never used git as being
  worth exactly as much as a correction from a thirty-year head of department.
  The index does not care who found the source.
- Settle disagreements by citing the primary document. Seniority is not
  evidence.
- Say "I do not know" and "I was wrong" freely. This whole project depends on
  people being comfortable doing that.

## Not accepted

- Personal attacks, harassment, or demeaning comments about someone's
  experience level, background, language, or the size of the venues they work
  in
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind
- Gatekeeping. "If you have to ask, you should not be here" is the opposite of
  the point.
- Publishing anyone's private information
- Sustained disruption of discussion

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues and pull requests, and anywhere
someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report concerns to the maintainers via a private message or by opening a
GitHub security advisory if you need it confidential. Reports are handled
discreetly. Maintainers may edit, hide, or delete contributions and may block
accounts, and will say what happened and why where it is appropriate to do so.

Adapted in spirit from the Contributor Covenant 2.1, rewritten to sound like
this industry rather than a policy document.
